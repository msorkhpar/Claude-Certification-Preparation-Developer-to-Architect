Level: Architect Professional · Module 94: Exam readiness 4 · Page 2 of 4 Exams: P1 to P7 (CCAR-P)

After this page you can use the Level 4 flashcards and the spaced-review question bank on a schedule, read a mock exam result by domain and by cause of the miss, and run a four-week plan that spends time where the marks are.

Written from the course's own revision files and the exam map, on 2026-10-04. The spaced-review schedule is the one module 11 introduced; the plan is this course's working method and the guides do not publish a method for preparing. The official exam guide and the Anthropic pages decide every fact about the exam, so check them before you rely on a number here.

Why it matters

Level 4 holds fifteen modules, and nobody keeps all of it in mind. The mock exam tells you how you stand, and the revision files tell you how to move. Without a routine, revision becomes rereading the pages that felt good the first time. With one, each session targets a named weakness, returns to it on a schedule, and is finished when the weakness stops appearing in the mock. Level 4 adds a difficulty of its own: its questions are scenarios, and a fact that you can recite is not yet a fact that you can apply to a scenario in a minute and a half.

Spaced review

Memory fades quickly and recovers with well-timed practice. The schedule is the same as in module 11 and is stored in the bank as intervals_days: review a new card or question on the day you meet it, then after 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30 days, each counted from the previous review. An item that you answer correctly moves to the next interval, and an item that you miss goes back to the first. Three habits make it work.

  • Answer before you look. Say the answer aloud, or write it, then turn the card. The effort of recall is what strengthens memory, and a look at the answer is not recall.
  • Keep sessions short and daily. Twenty minutes every day beats three hours on a Sunday.
  • Read the page behind a miss. A wrong answer on a card or an item names a page. Reread that section once, then retest it tomorrow.

Reading a mock result

A percentage hides what you need. Score by domain, using the table on the mock page, and keep an error log with one line for every miss. Write down three things: the question's number and domain, the page that the key names, and the cause.

CauseWhat it looks likeWhat to do
Did not knowYou have no memory of the factReread the page, make a card for the fact, add it to the schedule
MisreadYou knew it and answered another questionRead the ask first, the last sentence of the scenario; slow down on "first", "best" and "most likely"
Took a distractorYou knew the topic and chose a plausible mistakeRead the key's reasons for each wrong option, and name the mistake in your log
Wrong domainYou applied a rule of another layer to the scenarioName the failing layer before you choose, using the decision patterns of page 1

The distribution of causes tells you what to change. Mostly "did not know" in one domain is a study problem. Mostly "misread" everywhere is a technique problem, and no more reading of pages will fix it. "Wrong domain" is specific to scenario exams: the stem mentions retrieval, evaluation and governance in the same breath, and the item rewards the candidate who finds which of them carries the weight. The mock exam is not a conversion to the exam's score: the pass mark is a scaled score of 720 on a scale from 100 to 1,000, and no percentage is published for it. Use the domain figures to decide where to work, and not to predict the result.

A four-week plan

The plan assumes about forty minutes on most days and a little more at the weekend, and it assumes that you have studied Level 4 once. Change the numbers to your time, and keep the order.

  1. Week 1: baseline. Take the mock exam untimed, with no notes, and read every explanation. Fill the error log. Rank the domains by the weight of the domain times the share you missed. Start the flashcard schedule for the top two domains.
  2. Weeks 2 and 3: the weak domains, by weight. Work the review bank for the ranked domains, and reread the pages behind your misses. Make a card for every fact that you did not know. In the second week add the next domain on the list. Keep the daily flashcard session going; it is short, and it is the part that holds. Read module 93 once more in week 3: it is the one module that uses all seven domains together.
  3. Week 4: exam conditions. Take the other mock exam in one sitting of 120 minutes, with no notes and the pacing of page 1, after at least two weeks since the first sitting so that you answer from understanding and not from memory of the keys. Compare its domain scores with the first sitting. Spend the next three days on the domains that still lag.
  4. The last two days. Review flashcards and your error log only, and check the logistics of module 11, page 2: the name on the booking against the ID, the booking time, the system check or the journey.

If your first sitting already shows an even profile, shorten the plan and move the second sitting earlier. If it shows a gap in a heavy domain, add a week and begin with that domain's pages, not its cards: cards recall a fact, and a pattern needs the page.

What to do the day before

Stop studying new material. Read the error log once, the flashcards that were missed twice and the decision patterns on page 1. Sleep. The mock exam is practice, and one more sitting on the last night teaches you little and costs you rest.

Traps

  1. Rereading what you already know. The pages that felt easy are the ones you reread. Work the misses.
  2. Memorising the mock. The questions are the course's own and will not appear in the exam. What carries over is the reasoning, so learn why each wrong option fails.
  3. Treating "misread" as bad luck. A misread is a repeatable habit. Name it in the log and fix the habit.
  4. Skipping the schedule. A card reviewed once and never again is forgotten. The intervals are the method.

Cards

0 of 95 known

Cards written for this site from several pages of this level. Nothing here is graded, and what you mark is kept in this browser.

  1. Front

    What are the five rungs of the pattern ladder, lowest first?

    Back

    One call, an augmented call (retrieval, a tool or memory around it), a workflow (order fixed by code), an agent (the model picks the next step) and a team of agents. Choose the lowest rung that meets the requirement.

  2. Front

    What two questions settle most pattern choices?

    Back

    Is the path known? If you can write the steps down in order, code should run them. Does the work need the model to decide what to do next? Only then does autonomy earn its price.

  3. Front

    Name the five value pillars of the exam guide.

    Back

    Efficiency, transformation, productivity, cost and performance service levels. Each is a claim about value and needs a baseline and a measurement before the build starts.

  4. Front

    What does a needed accuracy follow from?

    Back

    Two costs: the error cost and the review cost. An item is worth a review when (1 - accuracy) x error cost is above the review cost; with 250 and 5 the break-even is 98 percent.

  5. Front

    Who answers for a decision that an automated workflow takes wrongly?

    Back

    The organisation that deploys it: deploying an automated decision means owning its outcomes. The provider supplies a capability, not accountability, so decisions that carry accountability stay with a person.

  6. Front

    Name three assumptions of a ten-user pilot that may fail at two thousand users.

    Back

    The inputs were typical, people covered the edge cases by hand, capacity was never close, reviewers could keep up, access was simple, the data did not drift. A roll-out plan lists each with a test and a trigger.

  7. Front

    Name the four stages every end-to-end design has.

    Back

    Input, processing, output and feedback. The feedback stage, the path by which evaluation and corrections change the system, is the one most proposals omit.

  8. Front

    What token multipliers does Anthropic report for agents and multi-agent systems against a chat?

    Back

    About 4 times for an agent and about 15 times for a multi-agent system. For a team to pay for itself, the value of the task has to be at least 15 chats.

  9. Front

    What three reasons must hold together before a team of agents is worth building?

    Back

    The parts are independent, the volume of reading exceeds one context, and the value pays the multiple of about 15 chats. A team that fails the first test is a worse single agent.

  10. Front

    Why can two correct components fail together, and what is the fix?

    Back

    An under-specified contract, especially for empty and error cases. For each boundary write four things: the input, the output, the empty and error cases, and who checks the result.

  11. Front

    What does a subagent receive, and what should it return?

    Back

    Only what its task description holds: objective, output format, tools and sources, boundaries. It returns a digest, the finding with its source in the agreed shape, and not a transcript.

  12. Front

    In the review rubric, how does the verdict follow the findings, and where does price come in?

    Back

    Any high finding gives reject, otherwise any medium gives revise, otherwise approve. Price enters last, and only among the designs that were not rejected: the cheapest of those wins.

  13. Front

    When is a retry safe for a call that changes something?

    Back

    When the tool can recognise the repeat: an idempotency key sent with the call and recorded together with the effect. The same key is used on every retry of one task, since it names the intention.

  14. Front

    What goes wrong if the idempotency key and its effect are saved in separate steps?

    Back

    A crash between the two leaves an effect with no key, or a key with no effect, so the next attempt either pays twice or never pays. Record both in one transaction.

  15. Front

    What are the three states of a circuit breaker?

    Back

    Closed (calls go through, and a set number of failures in a row opens it), open (calls are refused at once until a cooldown) and half-open (one probe goes through: success closes it, failure opens it again).

  16. Front

    Which failures should a runner retry, and which not?

    Back

    Transient ones that pass (a timeout, a rate limit, a lost response), up to a fixed limit. Fatal ones, such as a refused request, a missing permission or an invalid input, are never retried.

  17. Front

    Why is a fallback's answer not written to the checkpoint?

    Back

    A stored degraded answer makes the task look done, so no later run tries the primary again and the weaker answer becomes final by accident. It is used, marked as degraded and left out of the store.

  18. Front

    What does a second run do after a first run in which one task failed?

    Back

    It takes finished tasks from the checkpoint and calls agents only for what is left: the failed task and the tasks skipped because of it. A crash, unlike a failure, is not caught by the runner.

  19. Front

    In what order is a cached prefix built, and what breaks it?

    Back

    Tools, then system, then messages. Two requests share a cache entry only when everything up to the breakpoint is identical, so a changing value, even a timestamp, inside the prefix breaks every match.

  20. Front

    How does a prompt assembler treat variables, order and a missing value?

    Back

    Variables belong in dynamic modules only; the assembler decides the order, static modules first; a missing variable makes it refuse the request instead of sending the placeholder.

  21. Front

    What is the minimum cacheable prefix for Claude Sonnet 5.5, and what happens below it?

    Back

    512 tokens at the time of checking. Below the minimum the marking has no effect and reports no error, so count the prefix and read the cache figures in the usage.

  22. Front

    How does a budget drop context, and when does the assembler refuse?

    Back

    It drops the lowest-priority dynamic module first, and among equals the later one. Static modules are never dropped; if only static modules remain and still exceed the budget, the request is refused.

  23. Front

    What rule chooses a model for a workload?

    Back

    Among models whose tier is at least the workload's and whose latency is within the limit, take the cheapest, with ties broken by name. When none fits, the finding is that requirement and model list disagree.

  24. Front

    What does the prompting guide say about moving a technique to another model?

    Back

    Treat it as measured on that model and re-check it against your own evaluations before applying it to another. Keep an evaluation set that runs on every candidate model and prompt change.

  25. Front

    Which party is the data processor on the Claude API and on Amazon Bedrock?

    Back

    On the Claude API, Claude Platform on AWS and Claude in Microsoft Foundry it is Anthropic; on Amazon Bedrock and Google Cloud's Claude platform it is the cloud provider, with its own retention and region rules.

  26. Front

    What can be pinned for region on the Claude API, and what cannot?

    Back

    Inference geography can be pinned to us or global, at 1.1 times the price for us at the time of checking. There is no European pin, so a customer that needs Europe needs another platform.

  27. Front

    What is the effect of a model that requires 30-day retention on a zero-retention requirement?

    Back

    It cannot satisfy it: zero data retention excludes such models unless Anthropic expressly authorises it, so the model is part of the check and not only the platform.

  28. Front

    What separates the data of tenants on the Claude API?

    Back

    One workspace per tenant, with its own keys, limits and spend. A single shared workspace leaves the separation to application code alone, which is a finding for a multi-tenant requirement.

  29. Front

    What three properties make an identifier-tokenising layer work?

    Back

    The same value gets the same token, the vault that maps tokens to values stays local to the caller, and tokens are swapped back after the reply. Test it by planting identifiers of every kind.

  30. Front

    What are the floor and the ceiling of audit retention, and what outranks the ceiling?

    Back

    The floor is the minimum period records must be kept, the ceiling the maximum without a reason. An entry is purged only when it is more than the ceiling old, and a legal hold outranks the ceiling.

  31. Front

    What are the three rate limits, and how are they computed for a workload?

    Back

    RPM, ITPM and OTPM: requests, counted input tokens and output tokens per minute. Each is the peak rate times its token mix plus headroom, rounded up, compared with the tier; the one that fails first binds.

  32. Front

    Which input tokens count toward ITPM for most Claude models?

    Back

    Uncached input tokens and cache writes count; cache reads do not. Caching a stable prefix therefore relieves the limit as well as the bill, and can move a workload down a tier.

  33. Front

    What do 429 and 529 mean, and what does the design do?

    Back

    A rate-limit 429 says how long to wait: wait and retry with backoff. A spend-cap 429 has no wait time: do not retry, raise the limit and alert a person. A 529 is overload: retry with backoff or fall back.

  34. Front

    What does an internal gateway centralise, and what does it not do by itself?

    Back

    Credentials, routing, budgets and the record of spend. It does not reduce tokens, make a model faster or guarantee a lower bill; the savings come from caching, model choice and batching that it enforces.

  35. Front

    How does gateway admission treat a budget at its edges?

    Back

    A budget that is not positive blocks. A request that would bring spend over the budget blocks, and one that reaches it exactly does not. Reaching 80 percent warns, and exactly 80 percent warns.

  36. Front

    Why does showback round once, at the end?

    Back

    Rounding each usage row first gains or loses cents: two rows of 0.4 cent are 1 cent together and 0 apart. The total is divided by a million and rounded once, and a row with an unpriced model is refused.

  37. Front

    Where should a document be cut, and why does a fixed size fail?

    Back

    At the joints of its data: headings, clauses, rows, functions, speaker turns. A fixed size ignores meaning and can split a rule from its exception, leaving no chunk that holds the whole rule.

  38. Front

    How does a chunk come to stand alone?

    Back

    It carries context: from the structure (document title and section) or from a model-written sentence, usually 50-100 tokens, added before embedding and keyword indexing. Contextual retrieval cut failure rates in Anthropic's tests.

  39. Front

    Where does access control belong in retrieval, and what fails otherwise?

    Back

    Before the ranking, so a chunk the reader may not see is never a candidate. Filtering after taking the best k leaves a reader with fewer than k results while hidden chunks took the top places.

  40. Front

    Which mechanism answers a table question, an identifier question and a paraphrase question?

    Back

    A structured query by a tool for a table, a keyword index for an identifier such as an error code, an embedding index for a paraphrase, and both merged by rank when both kinds arrive.

  41. Front

    What does a correct re-index do with a changed document?

    Back

    It removes all the old chunks, then adds the new ones. Adding only the new chunks leaves a correct record of a past fact competing for the top places. Compare chunk versions with source versions to find stale ones.

  42. Front

    What four outcomes come from judging retrieval and generation separately?

    Back

    Ok (evidence retrieved, answer right), generation (evidence retrieved, answer wrong), retrieval (no evidence, answer wrong) and unsupported (answer right without evidence). The denominator of recall is every labelled question.

  43. Front

    Which mechanism fits a known path, one application, several clients and another agent?

    Back

    A direct call in code for a known path, a custom tool for one application, an MCP server when several clients reuse it, and agent-to-agent when the counterpart is an independent agent with its own goals and owner.

  44. Front

    What does a gateway give for credentials, and what is its cost?

    Back

    The provider key stays server-side and developers hold gateway credentials, so offboarding revokes one credential. The cost is that the gateway becomes infrastructure you operate.

  45. Front

    Whose rights should a call made by an agent for a user use?

    Back

    Both: allow the call only when the user holds the scope the tool needs and the agent does too. The agent's rights are a ceiling, not a licence, and a denial should say which side lacked the scope.

  46. Front

    What do the MCP guidelines say about token passthrough and scopes?

    Back

    Token passthrough is explicitly forbidden in the authorization specification. Scopes follow least privilege: start from read operations and raise the scope when a privileged operation is first attempted.

  47. Front

    How does the exam's sample item treat least privilege?

    Back

    Remove the refund and delete tools the role never needs from the configuration entirely. Logging and confirmation prompts are compensating controls, and a larger model does not change what the agent may do.

  48. Front

    When does the tool search tool pay, and what stays in the request?

    Back

    With 10 or more tools or definitions over 10k tokens; keep the 3-5 most used tools loaded. Every definition is still sent in the tools array, deferred ones carry defer_loading, and the prefix and caching are preserved.

  49. Front

    What question does each of metrics, events and traces answer?

    Back

    Metrics say that something changed, events say what happened on one occasion, and traces say where in the chain a request went wrong. Use metrics for the alarm, traces for the location, events for the detail.

  50. Front

    Why is tail-based sampling better than a random head-based share?

    Back

    It decides when the trace is complete and can look at the outcome, so it keeps errors, slow traces and complaints. A random one percent holds almost none of the rare failures.

  51. Front

    How do you find the layer that failed in a trace of agents?

    Back

    Take the failing spans and find the deepest one that no other failing span has as its parent. A failed span is a fact and a stale retrieval is a suspicion; blame retrieval only when nothing failed.

  52. Front

    What should not be logged, and which Claude Code variables open content?

    Back

    Keep ids, counts, timings, names and statuses; drop content fields. Claude Code redacts prompt text, tool input and tool content by default; OTEL_LOG_USER_PROMPTS, OTEL_LOG_TOOL_DETAILS and OTEL_LOG_TOOL_CONTENT open them.

  53. Front

    What is drift, and how is it checked?

    Back

    A move in a metric that no error announces. Store a baseline per metric, compare each current value and flag any move beyond a tolerance, in either direction; reset a baseline only at a change you decided on.

  54. Front

    What makes an alert mean something, and what raises the cost of telemetry?

    Back

    Alert on a change that lasts, over several windows in a row, and never hide a problem to quiet the pager. High-cardinality metric labels such as a conversation id raise the cost: put them in traces and events.

  55. Front

    Which five dimensions does the exam guide list for evaluation, and what must a criterion be?

    Back

    Accuracy, latency, cost, safety and security. A criterion must be one you can fail: specific and measurable, such as 95 percent of refund answers matching the policy on a fixed set.

  56. Front

    What should an evaluation set look like?

    Back

    Like the traffic: sample real usage keeping its proportions, add awkward cases on purpose and tag them. Start small, about 20 queries, because large effect sizes show up in a few cases.

  57. Front

    Which grader fits which output?

    Back

    Code grading for one correct form, a model grader with a rubric for free-form text (check its agreement with people first), and people for what neither catches. Judge an agent's outcome, not its path.

  58. Front

    Why does a table by segment beat a headline score?

    Back

    An average hides unequal failures. The table shows cases, accuracy and the cost of the errors for each type of case, so a system that fails the costly segment is exposed.

  59. Front

    What are the rules of an honest A/B test?

    Back

    Decide one metric and the sample before looking, use at least the minimum cases per arm (200 in the example), do not stop by who is ahead, and report the direction of a clear difference.

  60. Front

    What are the two keys of the shadow-run gate, and in what order is a wrong answer diagnosed?

    Back

    No protected segment may lose a case, and elsewhere losses must not outnumber gains. Diagnose in the cheapest order: retrieval and evidence first, then the prompt, and the model last.

  61. Front

    What are the four lifecycle states of a model, and how much notice is given?

    Back

    Active, legacy, deprecated and retired; requests to retired models fail. Anthropic gives at least 60 days' notice for publicly released models, and 'not sooner than' marks a date that may move later.

  62. Front

    Which five Claude Sonnet 5.5 settings return a 400?

    Back

    Thinking budgets, sampling parameters (temperature, top_p, top_k), assistant prefill, forced tool choice and thinking disabled. The fix is to stop sending the setting, not to change its value.

  63. Front

    Which migration changes throw no error?

    Back

    Adaptive thinking by default (read content blocks by type), max_tokens covering thinking, refusals needing handling, and a higher price per token with more tokens per text: recount and re-baseline cost.

  64. Front

    What are the five checks of the migration gate?

    Back

    Must-pass cases, protected segments, net change, cost against a budget (a rise exactly at the budget passes) and the nearest-rank 95th-percentile latency. Every failing check adds a reason.

  65. Front

    How is a staged roll-out stepped?

    Back

    Stages of 1, 5, 25 and 100 percent: hold while the stage has too few requests, roll back to zero when errors per thousand pass the limit, otherwise advance.

  66. Front

    Why does a migration have two deadlines?

    Back

    The date by which the new model must be ready, and the date after which the old one cannot be the fallback. Keep the previous model configured and tested until its own retirement, and start early.

  67. Front

    Name the four layers of controls.

    Back

    Input (screens, untrusted content in tool results), output (grounding check, refusal handling), action (least privilege, approvals) and monitor (drift, parity report, alerts). One layer's control does not cover another's.

  68. Front

    What does a control do when it fails: hold or proceed flagged?

    Back

    A control on the input or action layer, and any high-tier control, holds. Only an output or monitor control of the ordinary tier may proceed with a flag, so that the gap shows in the record.

  69. Front

    How is a high-consequence action routed, and what about confidence?

    Back

    To a person whatever the confidence. A low-consequence action goes out unreviewed only at a confidence of 95 or more and only if the source supports the answer; confidence is not evidence.

  70. Front

    What should a design do on a streamed refusal?

    Back

    Reset the context (remove or rephrase the turn that caused it) or retry on a different model. The stop_details fields can be null, so the user-facing message comes from the application.

  71. Front

    How does erasure work with an audit log that holds tokens?

    Back

    Remove the vault map from tokens to people. The audit entries stay as proof and can no longer be linked to anyone. The deadline (30 days in the example) is a tested threshold.

  72. Front

    How is fairness measured, and what duty of transparency travels with it?

    Back

    As a gap between groups on one metric in a parity report, with an owner. Tell every person who receives output that AI helped produce it, as part of the deployment, and show the basis of an answer.

  73. Front

    When is a requirement complete?

    Back

    When it has a number, a way to measure it and a person who owns it. 'Fast' and 'accurate' are not requirements because nobody can fail them.

  74. Front

    What are the rules for the options in a design record?

    Back

    At least four options, exactly one recommended, the recommended one the cheapest that meets the service levels, and every rejected option with a reason in a sentence.

  75. Front

    What four things does the trade-off statement tell, in order?

    Back

    What improves, what it costs, what is at risk and what is asked. Leaving out the downside is the common failure.

  76. Front

    What goes in the sponsor's summary, and how long is it?

    Back

    Money, risk and one decision, in plain words and at most 80 words: the monthly cost against the baseline, the weakest answers as right N times in 100, who decides them and the decision asked.

  77. Front

    How is a service level written and reported at its edge?

    Back

    As a target with a number, a unit and a direction, a measure and an owner. A ceiling is met below it and a floor above it; exactly at the limit is met, and a miss says by how much.

  78. Front

    What does each assumption of a pilot need in the record?

    Back

    A test and a stop trigger with a number in it, such as stopping when the review queue is older than 4 hours. A trigger like 'if it goes wrong' is not a trigger.

  79. Front

    What is the order of settings precedence in Claude Code, highest first?

    Back

    Managed settings, command line, project local, shared project, user. A key set at a higher level overrides the same key lower down; no user, project or local value overrides a managed one.

  80. Front

    How do list settings such as permission rules combine, and what stops the merge?

    Back

    They combine across files, with duplicates removed. allowManagedPermissionRulesOnly in managed settings makes the managed rules the only ones, and lower entries are ignored.

  81. Front

    Name keys that only managed settings read.

    Back

    allowManagedHooksOnly, allowManagedMcpServersOnly, strictKnownMarketplaces and disableSideloadFlags among others. A lower file that sets one has no effect.

  82. Front

    What is the difference between a managed model and availableModels?

    Back

    A managed model is a default that a developer can still change. The lock is availableModels, a list, which a managed list applies as it is.

  83. Front

    Which cap applies when several levels set maxEffortLevel?

    Back

    The lowest. A developer may lower the organisation's cap and nobody can raise it. disableClaudeAiConnectors set to true from any level also stands.

  84. Front

    What are the limits of server-managed settings?

    Back

    Teams and Enterprise only, edited by an Owner or Primary Owner; fetched at startup and hourly; the same for all users, per-group not yet supported; fail closed only with forceRemoteSettingsRefresh.

  85. Front

    Which two commands show why a policy is not applying?

    Back

    /status shows the Setting sources line, which names the managed source Claude Code selected, and claude doctor lists what it dropped.

  86. Front

    How are plugin sources and MCP servers restricted?

    Back

    strictKnownMarketplaces lists allowed sources (an empty list blocks all, the official one included), disableSideloadFlags rejects the sideloading flags, and with allowManagedMcpServersOnly only the managed allowedMcpServers list applies.

  87. Front

    What is the shape of an MCP allowlist entry?

    Back

    Exactly one of serverName, serverCommand or serverUrl; an entry with two keys is invalid. A denied server wins over an allowed one, so none is in both lists.

  88. Front

    How should spend limits and adoption be set for a Claude Code rollout?

    Back

    Usage credits on, with limits at organisation, group and member level that add up (the groups sum to the organisation limit). Take a baseline of four weeks and target outcomes such as time to merge, not lines accepted.

  89. Front

    What is the order of the claims assistant's chain?

    Back

    Tokenise identifiers, retrieve by the reader's rights, check the chunk's version, check the answer's quote against it, route (person, auto or review), then trace and keep. Each step answers one failure.

  90. Front

    What does the chain do with evidence from an old version of a document?

    Back

    It holds the request as stale evidence, naming the chunk and the current version. A question with no word in common with a readable document gets no evidence, and an unsupported quote is held.

  91. Front

    What does the trace of a request hold in the capstone?

    Back

    The request id, the chunk and its version, the outcome and the number of characters, and no text. Tail-based keeping stores the traces that were held or reached a person.

  92. Front

    What does the capstone's release gate do with a change that loses an answer in a protected segment?

    Back

    It returns no-go and names the segment, even when gains elsewhere outnumber the loss. After the fix the same change is a go with no loss and three gains.

  93. Front

    How does the launch review order its findings and reach a verdict?

    Back

    By severity (high, medium, low), then domain P1 to P7, then rule id. Any high finding gives reject, otherwise any medium gives revise, otherwise approve; low findings alone are approved.

  94. Front

    How should a written design review be arranged?

    Back

    The verdict first, then the scorecard (a count of findings per domain), then the findings in order, each with the evidence and the fix. The sponsor stops at the verdict and the scorecard.

  95. Front

    How does the review compute the accuracy a design needs?

    Back

    100 minus the review cost as a percent of the error cost, with that percent rounded up, never below 0, and 0 when an error costs nothing. For 250 and 5 it is 98; for 3 and 1 it is 66.
