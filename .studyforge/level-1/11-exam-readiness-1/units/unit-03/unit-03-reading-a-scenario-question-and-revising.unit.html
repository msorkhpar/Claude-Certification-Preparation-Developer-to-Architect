Level: Foundations · Module 11: Exam readiness 1 · Page 3 of 5 Exams: all

After this page you can read a scenario question for what it really asks, rule out the distractor types the exams use, and run a revision routine with the course's flashcards, review bank and mock exams.

Written from the exam guides' own descriptions of the item format and from the Associate guide's three sample questions (version 1.0, July 2026, read 2026-10-02), plus the course's own quiz-writing rules. The guides do not publish a technique for answering items; the method below is this course's working method, and it is marked as such.

Why it matters

Every exam in the family is made of scenario questions with one best answer and plausible alternatives. A candidate who knows the material can still lose marks by answering a question that was not asked, picking an option that is true but not best, or running out of time on the early items. Technique does not replace knowledge, but it protects it.

The idea

What a scenario item looks like

The guides describe items that are multiple-choice or multiple-response, and each item states how many responses to select. A typical item has three parts:

  1. A short situation: a person, a task, a constraint, sometimes a failure.
  2. The ask: "what is the best first step", "which approach fits", "what is the most likely cause".
  3. Options: one best answer, and others that are plausible to someone who has made a particular mistake.

The Associate guide's own three samples show the pattern: a summary with a confident-looking citation (verify against the source), a high volume of short replies where speed and cost matter (a faster, lower-cost model), and a spreadsheet of personal data under a restrictive policy (remove or anonymise before upload). Each is a small decision with a reasoned answer and three tempting errors.

A working method (the course's own)

  1. Read the ask first. Find the question sentence, then read the situation with it in mind. "First", "best" and "most likely" mean different things.
  2. Note how many answers to select. The item says. Check it again before you submit.
  3. Mark the constraint. Cost, speed, risk, policy, audience, a deadline: the constraint is what separates the best option from the merely possible one.
  4. Name the problem type before you read the options. Is it missing context, a wrong feature, a data-sensitivity problem, a verification gap, a model-choice trade-off? A quick label predicts what the answer looks like.
  5. Predict, then compare. Form a one-line answer, then look for it. If none matches, re-read the constraint.
  6. Rule out, with a reason each. For each option you reject, be able to say in a few words why it fails. If you cannot, you do not understand it yet.
  7. Pick the best, not the first correct-sounding. More than one option may be partly true. The best is the one that addresses the root cause at the right scale.
  8. Change an answer only for a reason. A new fact you noticed is a reason; a feeling is not.

The distractor families

Wrong options are written to be tempting. They fall into families, and spotting the family is often quicker than analysing the option:

FamilyWhat it doesTypical sound
Too much machinerySolves a simple problem with a heavy build"Build a custom pipeline", "switch to the largest model for everything"
A different problemFixes something real that is not the one described"Reword the prompt" when the data is missing
Self-verificationAsks the model to confirm itself"Ask Claude how confident it is"
A request in place of a guaranteeAdds an instruction where a check or control is needed"Tell Claude not to retain the data"
Going around the ruleGets the result by bypassing policy"Use a personal account", "it is only internal"
Right idea, wrong momentCorrect step done too early, too late or out of orderScheduling a task before it has been proved
The absoluteUses always, never or all"Always use the top tier"
True but not bestA correct statement that does not answer this questionA real feature that does not fit the constraint

The same few lessons answer a large share of Level 1 items, which is why the course repeats them:

  • Confidence is not evidence; check a claim that matters against the source of record.
  • Exact work belongs in code; a model approximates counts and sums.
  • A prompt is a request; what must hold every time belongs in a check or a control.
  • Change one thing at a time and re-run the same test inputs.
  • Match the model to the task; the biggest tier for everything wastes budget, the cheapest for everything under-serves.
  • Anonymise before you upload, and raise a policy conflict with the person who owns the policy.
  • Least reach, least action: give an assistant only the folders, tools and sites the task needs, and keep a person on consequential steps.

Time

At 120 minutes for 60 items you have about two minutes an item, and less if you want time to review. A sensible pattern is a first pass at roughly 90 seconds an item, answering what you know and keeping a note of the uncertain ones, then a second pass on the uncertain ones with the time left. The guides do not describe how the exam interface lets you move between items or mark them, so learn the interface from the exam provider's tutorial and do not assume a feature. The guides also do not describe any penalty for a wrong answer, so leave no item blank by choice.

All three are plain data files in this module's exercises folder, and course/README.md documents their format. Tags let you filter by Associate domain (AS1 to AS7) or by module.

Spaced review. Memory fades quickly and recovers with well-timed practice. A simple schedule: review a new card or question on the day you meet it, then after 1 day, 3 days, 7 days, 14 days and 30 days, moving a card to the next interval when you answer it right and back to the first when you do not. Short daily sessions beat long weekend ones. Answer from memory before you look, because recall effort is what strengthens memory.

A plan for the last fortnight.

  1. Take the first mock exam once, untimed, and mark each miss with its domain and the reason (did not know, misread, took a distractor).
  2. Study the weakest domains first, weighted by their share of the exam; domain 2 is the heaviest.
  3. Work the review bank for those domains on the spaced schedule, and read the page behind every miss.
  4. Take the second mock exam under exam conditions: 120 minutes for its 60 questions, no notes.
  5. In the last two days only review flashcards and your own error list, and check the logistics of page 2 (name matches ID, booking time, system test or travel).

Traps

  1. Answering a different question. Reading the situation and jumping to a familiar fix, without reading the ask or the constraint.
  2. Choosing the first option that is true. More than one can be true; the best one fits the constraint and the root cause.
  3. Cramming the night before. Spaced practice over weeks does more than one long session.

Cards

0 of 133 known

Cards written for this site from several pages of this level. Nothing here is graded, and what you mark is kept in this browser.

  1. Front

    What does a language model do at each step of generating text?

    Back

    It scores every token it knows for how likely it is to come next, one token is chosen and appended, and the loop repeats until the model finishes, reaches max_tokens or fills the context window.

  2. Front

    About how many English characters is one token for Claude?

    Back

    About 3.5 characters. The figure varies with the language, and newer tokenizers can produce about 30 percent more tokens for the same text.

  3. Front

    What counts toward the context window?

    Back

    The system prompt, every message (including tool results, images and documents), the tool definitions and the output Claude generates for the turn, including its thinking.

  4. Front

    Does the Messages API remember earlier turns?

    Back

    No. It is stateless: the code resends the whole history on every request. Chat apps manage the history for you.

  5. Front

    What is context rot?

    Back

    Accuracy and recall degrade as the token count grows, so a bigger window does not remove the need to curate what goes into it.

  6. Front

    What happens if the input alone is larger than the context window?

    Back

    The API returns a 400 invalid_request_error (prompt is too long) on every model.

  7. Front

    What does max_tokens limit?

    Back

    Only the output of a turn. Raising it does not enlarge the context window.

  8. Front

    Does temperature zero make Claude's output identical every time?

    Back

    No. The glossary says the results are not fully deterministic even at temperature 0, and the current models reject non-default temperature, top_p and top_k values.

  9. Front

    Name two sources of variation between runs of the same prompt.

    Back

    Sampling (a draw from a distribution that compounds across steps) and infrastructure differences between calls.

  10. Front

    What does the model know about recent events or your private data?

    Back

    Only what was in its training data up to the cutoff (June 2026 for the three large models, February 2025 reliable for Haiku 4.5). It has no clock, so supply dates, facts and private material in the context or through a tool. (checked 2026-10-02)

  11. Front

    Define hallucination and name two ways to reduce it.

    Back

    Fluent, confident output that is wrong or unsupported. Allow the model to say it does not know, ask for word-for-word quotes first, ask it to retract unsupported claims, or restrict it to the supplied documents. These reduce it but do not eliminate it.

  12. Front

    A prompt is a request. What does that imply?

    Back

    A sentence in a prompt makes a behaviour more likely but not certain, so anything that must hold every time belongs in code, such as a check, a validator or a hook.

  13. Front

    What is pretraining?

    Back

    Training a model on a very large unlabeled text corpus to predict the next token. The result continues text but is not yet good at following instructions.

  14. Front

    Does using Claude change its weights?

    Back

    No. Training changes weights; inference does not. Apparent learning within a conversation is the context window, and memory features put saved notes back into the context.

  15. Front

    Why are tokens not words?

    Back

    A tokenizer, built before pretraining, cuts text into frequent sub-word pieces. Common words may be one token, rare words are built from several smaller pieces.

  16. Front

    What does tokenization do to counting and spelling tasks?

    Back

    The model sees pieces rather than letters, so exact character counts and spelling work are approximate. Hand them to code.

  17. Front

    What does the glossary say about fine-tuning Claude?

    Back

    Fine-tuning is further training on additional data. The Claude API does not currently offer fine-tuning, a statement about the Claude API only.

  18. Front

    What is RLHF?

    Back

    Reinforcement learning from human feedback: humans rank example answers and reinforcement learning encourages the model to prefer outputs like the higher-ranked ones.

  19. Front

    What are the two phases of Constitutional AI?

    Back

    A supervised phase of self-critique and revision against written principles, then reinforcement learning from AI feedback using a preference model.

  20. Front

    What does HHH stand for, and what is it?

    Back

    Helpful, honest, harmless. It is a research framework that informs how Claude is trained and a goal for behaviour, not a guarantee about any single answer.

  21. Front

    Explain attention in one sentence.

    Back

    When choosing the next token, every position in the context can look at every earlier position and weigh how relevant it is; it is a limited resource spread across the context.

  22. Front

    Name two things training does not give a model.

    Back

    A guarantee that statements are true, knowledge after the cutoff, access to your files or today's date, memory of earlier conversations, or perfect arithmetic and counting.

  23. Front

    Where is everything a model uses stored?

    Back

    In the context window or in the weights. There is no third place, so put a fact you have into the window.

  24. Front

    Which four current models does the course use, and what are their API ids?

    Back

    Claude Fable 5.1 (claude-fable-5-1), Opus 5.5 (claude-opus-5-5), Sonnet 5.5 (claude-sonnet-5-5) and Haiku 4.5 (claude-haiku-4-5-20251001, alias claude-haiku-4-5), as read on 2026-10-02.

  25. Front

    What are the context windows of the four models?

    Back

    1M tokens for Fable 5.1, Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5, and 200K for Haiku 4.5. (checked 2026-10-02)

  26. Front

    What are the list prices per million input and output tokens?

    Back

    Fable 5.1 $10 and $50, Opus 5.5 $4 and $20, Sonnet 5.5 $2 and $10, Haiku 4.5 $1 and $5, as read on 2026-10-02.

  27. Front

    Describe the efficiency-first and capability-first strategies.

    Back

    Efficiency first starts on the fast, low-cost tier and moves up only for a gap you can name. Capability first starts from the strongest option and works down through lower effort or smaller models. Both end in measurement on your own data.

  28. Front

    What is effort, and why does it matter?

    Back

    A setting that trades intelligence for latency and cost within one model. The documentation says tuning effort is often a better lever than switching models.

  29. Front

    What are two multi-model designs for mostly easy work with occasional hard cases?

    Back

    An executor that escalates hard decisions to an advisor, and an orchestrator that hands bulk work to lower-cost workers.

  30. Front

    What do batch requests and prompt cache reads change about price?

    Back

    Batch API requests are 50 percent cheaper, and cache reads cost a fraction of the base input price.

  31. Front

    Which surface fits a non-developer with recurring work and reference files?

    Back

    The Claude apps with a Project. Building an API integration would add cost and upkeep for nothing.

  32. Front

    Do a Project's instructions reach your own API integration?

    Back

    No. Instructions live where they are read: Project instructions in the app, memory files and settings in Claude Code, the system prompt in the API.

  33. Front

    Why must you pin model ids and watch retirement dates?

    Back

    Every Claude model id is a pinned snapshot, and ids retire (Haiku 4.5 not before 2026-10-15). A hard-coded id with a retirement date needs a migration plan. (checked 2026-10-02)

  34. Front

    Do model ids and features match across the cloud platforms?

    Back

    No. The same model family has a different id on each platform, and access and some features differ, so read the platform's own model page.

  35. Front

    Name three limits of Claude's vision.

    Back

    It cannot name people in images, counts of many small objects are approximate, it is not designed for complex diagnostic scans such as CTs or MRIs, it cannot tell whether an image is AI-generated, and it only understands images and cannot create them.

  36. Front

    How are image tokens counted?

    Back

    Each 28 by 28 pixel patch is one visual token, up to a per-model cap.

  37. Front

    What is the fix for exact arithmetic, exact counts and string edits?

    Back

    Code or a tool, such as a calculator or code execution. Prompting harder does not change that the model works on tokens.

  38. Front

    How is thinking billed, and what else does it use?

    Back

    Thinking tokens are billed as output tokens, count toward max_tokens and use space in the context window.

  39. Front

    What are the default effort levels?

    Back

    High for Fable 5.1 and Sonnet 5.5, medium for Opus 5.5, as read on 2026-10-02.

  40. Front

    What is the main safety point about computer use?

    Back

    A screen is untrusted input that can carry injected instructions, so run it on an isolated machine without secrets and with a person confirming consequential steps.

  41. Front

    List three needs that should not go to a model alone.

    Back

    An exact repeatable result, a rule you can state in one line, a fact in your database, a high-stakes decision, data that must not leave your boundary, or anything that must happen every time.

  42. Front

    Where should long documents sit in a prompt, and where the question?

    Back

    Near the top, with the query at the end, which the documentation says can improve results by up to 30 percent.

  43. Front

    What is the check for reasoning that decides something important?

    Back

    An independent check, such as a test, a calculation in code or a second reader, because a wrong step makes everything after it wrong.

  44. Front

    Name the four working habits of module 5.

    Back

    Delegate, describe, discern and take responsibility. They run as a loop in which what you discern shapes the next description.

  45. Front

    Which steps stay with a person or with code?

    Back

    Judgment that carries accountability, exact work (money, counts, identifiers), anything touching data that policy keeps away from the tool, and anything hard to reverse.

  46. Front

    What three things does a good description cover?

    Back

    The product (what you want), the process (how to go about it) and the performance (how Claude should behave), plus the goal, context, constraints and an example when style matters.

  47. Front

    What four questions do you ask of any output?

    Back

    Is it accurate? Is it complete? Is it fair? Does it fit the audience?

  48. Front

    Give the four-step checking routine.

    Back

    Mark the claims that would hurt if wrong, check them against the source of record, route high stakes to a human reviewer, then adapt for the audience.

  49. Front

    Is asking Claude how sure it is a verification?

    Back

    No. Self-reported confidence is not a measure of accuracy; check against the source of record.

  50. Front

    What should you do with a declared gap such as data unavailable?

    Back

    Keep it and decide what to do about it. Filling it with an estimate or deleting the row hides the gap.

  51. Front

    Why iterate rather than re-roll?

    Back

    Regenerating the same request is a second guess. Name what is wrong, change one thing in the description and compare.

  52. Front

    Name the consequences of owning the result.

    Back

    Accountability for errors, disclosure of the AI's part when it matters, data care, and attention to fairness and impact.

  53. Front

    What are the three parts of a fair message about Claude's value and limits?

    Back

    What it speeds up, what still needs a person, and how you will know it works.

  54. Front

    What makes an instruction clear and direct?

    Back

    A verb for the task, a named deliverable (form, length, reader), constraints you could check afterwards, and the instruction stated as an instruction.

  55. Front

    Why give the purpose behind an instruction?

    Back

    A reason lets the model handle cases the rule did not list, so the instruction generalises.

  56. Front

    What are the limits of role prompting?

    Back

    A role is a request and not a credential, it needs a task to be useful, and it is not a safeguard.

  57. Front

    Why use tags, and is position a boundary?

    Back

    Tags mark kinds of content and keep data apart from instructions. Position is not a boundary: text placed above the instructions can still read as an instruction, so tag the data and say it is data.

  58. Front

    Zero-, one- and multi-shot: when each, and how many examples?

    Back

    Zero-shot for common easy tasks, one-shot to show a format once, multi-shot for subtle boundaries or unusual formats. The documentation suggests 3 to 5 relevant, diverse examples in a consistent format.

  59. Front

    Can you prefill the assistant's reply on current models?

    Back

    No. From the 4.6 models on, prefilled responses on the last assistant turn are no longer supported. Use structured outputs, a tool with enum values, direct instructions or code to strip stray text.

  60. Front

    What is the rule for output formats a program reads?

    Back

    Specify the format in the prompt, and check it in code, because a prompt makes a format likely but not certain.

  61. Front

    What three preconditions does the documentation set before prompt engineering?

    Back

    A clear definition of success criteria, a way to test against them and a first draft prompt.

  62. Front

    Give the stages for a complex request.

    Back

    Gather facts or quotes, reason from the gathered facts only, write the deliverable, then check it against the criteria.

  63. Front

    Name an emphasis for each task type.

    Back

    Analysis: material, question and evidence. Research: sources named and gaps declared. Drafting: audience, tone and an example. Brainstorming: breadth first, then a second pass to judge.

  64. Front

    What are the four causes of a weak output?

    Back

    Missing context, ambiguity, the wrong feature and the wrong model. Two further sources to rule out: a hallucination and a format failure.

  65. Front

    What is the controlled-fix method?

    Back

    Fix an evaluation set, change one thing, re-run the whole set, keep the change only if it helped and nothing else got worse, and record it.

  66. Front

    What order does optimisation follow?

    Back

    First reach the quality bar, then find the cheaper way to hold it: a shorter prompt, reuse of context, a smaller tier or lower effort proved on the set, and exact steps moved into code.

  67. Front

    What is a Project?

    Back

    A self-contained workspace with its own chat histories and knowledge base. Free accounts can hold up to five. (checked 2026-10-02)

  68. Front

    What is the difference between a Project's instructions and its knowledge?

    Back

    Instructions say how to work and apply to every chat in the Project. Knowledge is the reference material that says what is true.

  69. Front

    What is RAG mode in a Project?

    Back

    On paid plans, when a Project nears the context limit, Claude retrieves the relevant parts and expands the Project's capacity. (checked 2026-10-02)

  70. Front

    What do the Project permission levels allow?

    Back

    Can view is read-only access to contents, knowledge and instructions. Can edit also changes instructions, knowledge and access. Treat sharing as publishing.

  71. Front

    How does a synced Drive document differ from an uploaded file?

    Back

    A synced document follows its source and shows the latest version, while an uploaded file is a copy as of the upload. Sync reads text only.

  72. Front

    How does memory relate to Projects?

    Back

    Each Project has its own memory space and summary. Memory holds preferences and context and is not a substitute for a knowledge file. Incognito is only outside Projects.

  73. Front

    How do you keep a Project's sources current and unambiguous?

    Back

    Name an owner and a review date, keep one source of record, and delete superseded versions rather than asking Claude to prefer the newer one.

  74. Front

    How do you version and vet the files that go into a Project?

    Back

    Put a version or date in the name of files you control and keep earlier versions outside the Project. Judge each source first: final over draft, primary over summary, clean over scan.

  75. Front

    How do you test a change to Project instructions and spot a stale source?

    Back

    Change one thing at a time and re-run the same few test requests. Ask Claude to cite the file it used, which shows at once when it used an old one.

  76. Front

    When does Claude create an artifact, and how are artifacts shared?

    Back

    When content is significant and self-contained, typically over 15 lines. Artifacts are private by default and can be shared or published.

  77. Front

    What is the exam warning about artifact storage?

    Back

    Storage is text only, up to 20 MB, and personal or shared. Check whether the artifact uses shared storage before entering anything sensitive. (checked 2026-10-02)

  78. Front

    What are skills and how does Claude load them?

    Back

    Folders of instructions, scripts and resources that Claude loads dynamically through progressive disclosure, so only relevant skills enter the context.

  79. Front

    What is the security rule for skills?

    Back

    Install skills only from trusted sources and review their code and resources. The named risks are prompt injection and data exfiltration.

  80. Front

    When do you use Research, and what does it need?

    Back

    For open, broad questions needing many linked searches with citations. It needs a paid plan and web search turned on, and it can use limits faster. (checked 2026-10-02)

  81. Front

    How do you choose between inline, artifact and structured data?

    Back

    Short answers go inline, deliverables someone will open, edit or send are artifacts, and data another program reads is structured data.

  82. Front

    State the connector permission rule.

    Back

    Claude inherits each person's permissions from the connected service, so a connector cannot give more access than the person already has.

  83. Front

    What settings can control a connector's tools?

    Back

    Each tool can be always allowed, needs approval or blocked, and on Team and Enterprise plans an owner must enable connectors first. (checked 2026-10-02)

  84. Front

    What is the connector governance checklist?

    Back

    Connect only what the work needs, prefer the directory, read scopes before approving, separate read from write, review the list on a schedule and keep the source of record clean.

  85. Front

    When is Cowork the right choice rather than chat?

    Back

    When there is a clear deliverable and the work touches your files or tools. Chat is for one-off questions and turn-by-turn steering.

  86. Front

    Where do Cowork tasks run, and what does local file access need?

    Back

    Tasks run in the cloud and can continue when your computer is offline. Local files, browser and computer automation need the desktop app open and connected. (checked 2026-10-02)

  87. Front

    What are the Cowork approval modes?

    Back

    Manual asks before every action. Auto has Claude check each action for safety but still runs on its own. (checked 2026-10-02)

  88. Front

    Name safe-use habits for Cowork.

    Back

    Use a dedicated folder, avoid financial documents and credentials, use Manual for sensitive or hard-to-undo actions, watch for unexpected access, start scheduled tasks with low-risk work and trust only the sites you give it.

  89. Front

    What does Cowork's isolated cloud environment not limit?

    Back

    It protects your network, but it does not limit what Claude reads or does through connected tools.

  90. Front

    What is Claude in Chrome?

    Back

    A browser extension in the Chrome side panel on paid plans that reads and acts on pages with your logins. It is not supported on other browsers or on mobile. (checked 2026-10-02)

  91. Front

    List Claude in Chrome's permission modes.

    Back

    Manually approve, Automatically approve, and Skip all approvals, where nothing checks the actions. Downloads, sensitive inputs and authorisations still need approval, and purchases and permanent deletions are off limits. (checked 2026-10-02)

  92. Front

    What does the Chrome safety page say about HIPAA and regulated data?

    Back

    It is not available to HIPAA organisations, and it is not recommended on pages with regulated data. (checked 2026-10-02)

  93. Front

    What is Claude for Microsoft 365?

    Back

    Add-ins for Excel, PowerPoint, Word and Outlook from AppSource. Each has its own instructions and works on open files, and cross-app mode lets one conversation read one app and write in another. (checked 2026-10-02)

  94. Front

    What should you know about the add-ins' audit and retention?

    Back

    Inputs and outputs are deleted within 30 days, chat history is stored in the browser, they do not inherit custom retention settings and they are not in Enterprise audit logs, though the Compliance API can include them. (checked 2026-10-02)

  95. Front

    What are the Excel add-in's limits?

    Back

    Not recommended for final client deliverables without human review, audit-critical calculations without verification, or highly sensitive data without controls. Macros, VBA and data tables are unsupported, and only trusted spreadsheets should be used. (checked 2026-10-02)

  96. Front

    What is Claude Tag?

    Back

    Claude working in Slack channels and DMs on Team and Enterprise plans, in public beta as read on 2026-10-02. Channel work is visible to all and billed to the organisation, while a DM uses your own account and connectors.

  97. Front

    How is Claude Tag administered?

    Back

    An Owner pairs the Slack workspace, sets a monthly spend limit and gives Claude its own accounts in other tools with narrow roles. It is not available under zero data retention or customer-managed encryption. (checked 2026-10-02)

  98. Front

    What is the pattern for choosing a surface?

    Back

    Least reach, least action, and most visibility to the right people. Ask about the work's shape, what it must reach, who must see it and how much can go wrong.

  99. Front

    Name the 4D framework and its loops.

    Back

    Delegation, Description, Discernment and Diligence. Description and Discernment form the inner loop, and Delegation and Diligence frame it from outside.

  100. Front

    Back

    On code and user experience: run and test the output, since plausible code can do the wrong thing.

  101. Front

    What should a student not delegate?

    Back

    The thinking the course exists to build. Use Claude as a learning partner and follow the rules about help and disclosure.

  102. Front

    What is taught, how it is assessed and the fairness of grades, plus student data. Check accuracy, bias and alignment to the outcome.

  103. Front

    What is the key check for a non-profit using Claude?

    Impact claims, statistics and funder requirements against the source, and beneficiary and donor data classified and anonymised first.

    Prices, dates and promises against policy, with a short list of facts verified before anything reaches a customer, since the owner has no second reviewer.

  105. Front

    What starting point does the creative-work course recommend?

    Back

    A deliberate starting point of no AI, adding it only where analysis shows it earns a place. Delegate production, keep authorship and be open about it.

  106. Front

    What are the three parts of the Usage Policy?

    Universal usage standards, high-risk use case requirements, and additional guidelines for chatbots, minors, agentic use and MCP servers. The version read had an effective date of 15 September 2025. (checked 2026-10-02)

  107. Front

    What two safeguards apply to high-risk uses?

  108. Front

    Legal, healthcare, insurance, finance, employment and housing, academic testing and admissions, and media or journalism.

  109. Front

    Name the course's four data classes and a rule for each.

    Public (free use), internal (approved tools), confidential (approved tools, need to know, often de-identified) and regulated (not allowed unless policy and contract permit; anonymise first).

  110. Front

    Rank anonymising techniques.

    Back

    Aggregate, remove direct identifiers, replace with codes, generalise values, and remove unread free text. Check for re-identification.

  111. Front
